# Business details first

Run this before the research and before the brief. The business details are the facts every page, the Google profile and every listing copy from: the name, the phone, the email, the web address, the hours, the service area. Collect them once, check them once, and read them from one place from then on. On previous builds, details typed from memory into each page ended up spelt three different ways.

## Find the details

Look in the project folder for the user's details sheet: a spreadsheet, CSV or document with "details" in its name, or a `docs/nap.md`. If there is one, it is the source of truth. If there is none, create `business-details.md` with the fields below and ask the user for them in one short list.

## The fields

- **Business name:** exactly as it will appear on the site. No keywords or city added that are not part of the real name.
- **Registered legal name and business number** (in Australia, the ABN).
- **Main service and main city.**
- **Website:** the production domain.
- **Phone:** see "Not yet is an answer" below.
- **Email:** one address used for this site only. A free mail account made for the site (for example Gmail) is fine, and is what the Google account and Business Profile need later. An address on the site's own domain can come later.
- **Opening hours:** the real hours the phone is answered or enquiries are handled.
- **Service area:** the suburbs or regions really covered. On a lead network site this is the buyer's area; with no buyer yet, the areas the user plans to launch with.
- **Descriptions:** short, profile length and long. Leave these blank at this step.

Who does the work, and whether a buyer is signed, are settled in the brief, not here. Do not ask about them at this step. If the sheet has rows it marks for a later stage (for example profile categories or a verification address), leave them alone.

## Check, then ask once

1. Read every field. List what is filled in, what is blank and anything that does not line up: a name that differs from the domain or from what the user said, a phone number in the wrong format for the country, an email that is the user's personal address or is shared with another site, hours missing, a service area wider than a buyer could cover.
2. Ask for the blanks and the fixes in one short list. Do not ask for anything the sheet or the user's request already answers.
3. Write the answers back to the sheet if you can edit it, keeping its formulas and layout. If you cannot, give the user the exact values to type in. The value cell holds the value only. Anything that qualifies it ("lodged, not issued", "exact suburbs still to choose") goes in the notes column beside it.
4. Report in a few lines: complete, or what is still open.

## Not yet is an answer

- A user who has not bought a tracking number yet says so. Leave their "not yet" in the sheet as they wrote it. In the brief and the copy, treat it as `TO CONFIRM` and carry a clearly marked placeholder. Never invent a number, and put "swap in the real phone number" on the launch checklist (in WordPress mode, in `to-confirm.md`).
- The same goes for a business number that is lodged but not issued, and for a service area that waits on a buyer.
- Buyer facts (licence, reviews, job photos, a guarantee) do not belong in the details. They are handled in the brief as `TO CONFIRM WITH BUYER`.

## Descriptions come after the brief

Do not write the descriptions at this step: written before the research they are generic. Once the brief is agreed, draft a short description (one sentence), a profile description (750 characters or fewer, kept for the day a Google Business Profile is created) and a long description (two or three short paragraphs, 1,000 characters or fewer, for directory listings), using only facts in the brief and the business model wording from it. Leave out anything marked `TO CONFIRM`. Give each with its character count, and write them into the details sheet or hand them to the user to paste in.

## From here on

Every later step reads the name, phone, email, web address, hours and service area from the details, character for character. If one changes, change the details first, then the site, then every listing.

If the user asked only to check their details, stop here.
