# Conversion review and claims check

Two short passes that sit beside the scorecard. Run them when the user asks why a page is not converting, asks for a page to be reviewed "as a customer", or before any launch.

## The three-customer review

Read the home page and the main service page as a stranger would, on a phone-sized view if you can. Do it three times, as three different people:

1. **In a hurry.** On a phone, with an urgent problem, deciding in under a minute.
2. **Comparing quotes.** Collecting three prices for a planned job, sensitive to cost and to what is included.
3. **Cautious.** Let down by a tradesperson before, looking for reasons not to trust this one.

For each person report three things, briefly:

- What they were looking for.
- Where they hesitated, quoting the exact element or sentence.
- What would have made them enquire.

Then check the first screen on a small phone against four things that must be visible without scrolling: what the business does, where, the main action, and one true proof point.

Finish with the **five changes that would help most, in order**, each with the page, the section and the exact change. Stop at five.

Rules for this review:

- Never suggest adding a claim the business cannot prove. If the fix for a hesitation is proof the business does not have, say what proof to go and get.
- Do not suggest a second call to action competing with the main one. Suggest making the main one clearer.
- Opinion is not data. Once the site has traffic, the form step numbers and Search Console say where people really leave; say so, and prefer them.

## The claims check

A lead-gen site is trusted only as far as its claims are true, and untrue claims are the most expensive mistake to find after launch. List every claim a customer could check, on every page and in every image:

- Reviews, star ratings and review counts
- Licence, registration and insurance details
- Years in business, numbers of jobs or customers
- Guarantees and response times
- Team, staff and "our" wording that implies who does the work
- Prices and "from" prices
- Awards, memberships and "as seen in" logos
- Photos presented as real jobs or real people

For each claim record where it appears, and its source: the brief, a document the owner supplied, or "no source". Flag separately anything you wrote yourself that the owner did not supply.

Then, for a lead network, check the disclosure: the footer, the about page and the quote form all say enquiries go to a local contractor who does the work, and nothing elsewhere on the site contradicts it.

Report every "no source" item as a liability to fix or remove before launch. Do not soften it.
