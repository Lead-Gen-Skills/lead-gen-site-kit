# Google Ads for a lead-gen site

Ads are the bridge while search rankings build: leads this week, at a cost the lead price can carry. Build one simple search campaign and keep it honest. This is not a full ads course, and the screens change often, so tell the user what to choose and why, not where every button is.

You cannot see the user's account. Work from what they paste or export, and say when you are estimating.

## Step 1: will it pay

Before anything is built, do the sum with the user's own click cost estimates and lead price:

```
cost per lead = click cost / share of clicks that enquire
```

Show it at one in ten and at one in five clicks enquiring. Say which keywords work at each rate and what daily budget would bring about one lead a day. If the sum does not work on paper it will not work in the account; some trades have clicks too expensive for a new site, and those wait for rankings.

## Step 2: conversions first

No spend until Google Ads can see which clicks became leads. Three conversions: the quote form successfully sent, a tap on the phone number on the site, and a call made straight from the ad. Have the user send a test and confirm it registers. Also tell them to start advertiser verification early; it can take days.

## Step 3: build the campaign

Read `references/campaign-build.md` and produce the campaign from the project's brief, keyword map and page plan: ad groups by service, keywords, the landing page for each ad group, a negative keyword list, headlines and descriptions within length limits, and the extras. Use only claims that are in the brief.

## Step 4: the settings that protect the budget

Give the user this list to apply, with the reason for each:

1. **Search only.** Turn off the display network and search partners for this campaign.
2. **Location:** the service area only, set to people who are in the area, not people interested in it. This one setting saves more wasted spend than any other.
3. **Schedule:** the hours someone will answer the phone.
4. **Match types:** phrase and exact to start. Hold broad match until there is conversion data.
5. **Budget:** a daily cap the user is comfortable losing while learning.
6. **Bidding:** a simple strategy with a maximum click cost taken from the sum in step 1. Once the campaign has a few dozen conversions, a strategy that bids for conversions usually does better.
7. **Recommendations:** ignore automatic suggestions that raise budgets or add broad keywords until checked against the user's own numbers.

## Step 5: the weekly review

Ask for the last 7 days of the search terms report and the keyword report, and the lead price. Return:

- Searches to add as negative keywords, with the reason.
- Good searches to add as keywords.
- Any keyword that has spent more than twice the lead price with no conversion, to pause.
- Cost, conversions and cost per lead for the week, against the lead price.

Then remind the user to compare the conversions with the real leads in their lead software. A campaign that reports conversions but delivers wrong numbers and spam is not working.

Small numbers jump around. Decide on weeks, not days, and say so when the data is thin.

## A small demand test

For a user who wants proof before building a full site: the same campaign on the three to five most exact keywords, one city, a low daily cap, for one week, pointed at a single simple page with a form and a phone number. It buys the real click cost, how many people search, and whether they enquire. They need somewhere for the enquiries to go before it starts.

## Rules

- Everything in an ad must be true and match the landing page. No claims the site cannot back up.
- For a lead network, ads describe arranging a local contractor, not doing the work.
- Never promise results, a cost per lead or a return.
- Local services ads that require a trade licence are run by the licensed business under its own name, not by a lead network.
