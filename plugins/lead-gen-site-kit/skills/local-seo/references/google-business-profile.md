# Google Business Profile

For a local service business the profile is usually the biggest lead source, and the map pack is where most local clicks go.

## How Google ranks local results

Google names three factors:

1. **Relevance.** How well the profile matches the search. You control it with the primary category, services, description and the website the profile links to.
2. **Distance.** How close the business is to the searcher. For a service-area business this is anchored to the verified address. You cannot move it, and you must not try to fake it.
3. **Prominence.** How well known and trusted the business is: review count, quality and recency, profile completeness, activity, citations and links.

Relevance and prominence are the ones you can move. Set up relevance once, then work prominence every week.

## Before you start

- **Has the owner accepted the risk?** For a lead network, read the "Lead networks and the profile" section of the `local-seo` skill to the owner first. A profile needs a real address the owner can confirm, in the area it serves.
- **One Google account per site.** Create the profile from an account used only for that site.
- **Is this the right listing?** If the profile was created for a previous business name, Google's rule is that a rebrand must keep the same proper nouns and services. A different business name generally means marking the old listing closed and creating a new one. Reviews stay attached to the listing that received them and do not move.
- **Never move the verification address** to look closer to the city centre. It is a common suspension trigger.

## Verification

Postcards are now rare. Google usually asks for a video, recorded on a phone in one continuous take through the profile app. Prepare the owner for it:

- **Where:** an address the owner can confirm. Their own, or, with written agreement, a second address the contractor who takes the leads works from. A contractor's real premises is the most durable choice.
- **What the video generally needs to show**, in one take: where it is (street sign, the building, the surroundings), that the business exists (tools, equipment, a work vehicle, business registration), and that the person has access (unlocking a door, a vehicle or a storage area). Google's instructions on the day take priority over this list.
- **Show only what is true.** No staged signage, no borrowed vehicles, no stand-ins. If filming at the contractor's premises, film their real equipment with them present.
- **Have the details consistent first:** the same name, address and phone on the site and in the master details block.
- **If it is rejected:** read the reason, fix that one thing, record again. After a second rejection, use the support form and attach documents.

Never use a bought profile, an invented unit number or a pre-recorded upload passed off as live.

## Phase 0: foundations (one time)

Everything the profile asks for should already sit in the details sheet: the basics on its Details tab, and the categories, links, services, products and first posts on its Google profile tab (see `build-site` `references/00-business-details.md`). Copy each value from there, character for character. If a field is blank, fill it in the sheet first, then copy it.

- [ ] Verify the profile (see above).
- [ ] Name exactly matches the master details block. No keywords or city added to the name.
- [ ] Website set to the matching location page (or the home page; test which performs better).
- [ ] **Primary category**: the single biggest relevance signal. Pick the closest category competitors in the top three use, checked on Maps for the head term.
- [ ] Service area: only suburbs and regions genuinely serviced.
- [ ] Hours, plus special hours for public holidays.

## Phase 1: complete every field

- [ ] Secondary categories, only for services genuinely offered.
- [ ] Description (up to 750 characters): what you do, where, the precise guarantee, the business model. No URLs, prices or calls to action in the description. It must match the site's claims and disclosure.
- [ ] Services (aim for ten), each with a description of up to 300 characters, Google's limit, and a "from" price matching the site's pricing table.
- [ ] Products: one for each main service, with a real photo, a short description and a button to its service page. Products show as cards near the top of the profile, so fill them even for a service business.
- [ ] Attributes that apply (online estimates, onsite services, languages).
- [ ] Opening date (the real one).
- [ ] Booking or quote link pointing at the funnel with its own tracking, e.g. `?utm_source=gbp&utm_campaign=[location]`.
- [ ] Messaging only if someone will reply fast. Slow replies hurt.

## Phase 2: photos and video

- [ ] Square logo and landscape cover.
- [ ] Before and after job photos (aim for 10 or more to start), team and vehicle photos.
- [ ] Prepare each with the `optimize-images` skill: keyword filename, geotag.
- [ ] Add a few new photos every week.

## Phase 3: reviews

The biggest ongoing lever. See `reviews.md`.

## Phase 4: posts

- [ ] Start with one post for each main service, each linking to its service page.
- [ ] Then post weekly, taking turns through the service pages and suburb pages, with offers and tips in between. Each with a real photo and a button to that page tagged `utm_source=gbp`.
- [ ] Example tip post: "The number one reason [common problem] happens is [cause]. Here is how we sort it." (with a before and after)

## Phase 5: questions and answers

Google is removing the Q&A section from profiles, so do not spend time writing questions there. Put the common questions and their answers on the site's FAQ instead.

## Ongoing

- [ ] Monthly: record calls, website clicks, direction requests and searches (direct, discovery, branded) from the profile insights.
- [ ] Monthly: search the head term on Maps from the city centre and note your position.
- [ ] Keep hours, photos and services current. An active, complete profile outranks a stale one.
- [ ] Also set up **Bing Places** and **Apple Business Connect** with identical details. Both are free and feed Copilot, Apple Maps and Siri.

## A second profile

Rankings fade with distance from the verified address, so one profile rarely covers a whole city. A second profile is only worth adding when there is a second real address that can be verified, and after the first profile is producing leads. Give it its own phone number and link it to its own location page. Every extra profile is extra risk and upkeep.

## If the profile is suspended

1. Do not create a replacement profile, and do not rush edits into the suspended one. Both make it worse.
2. Read Google's guidelines against the profile and fix any mismatch. The usual causes are the name, the address type and the category.
3. Gather proof: business registration, a utility bill or lease for the address, photos of the premises and equipment.
4. Submit the reinstatement form once, with the proof.
5. Tell the contractor who takes the leads, and say what is being done.
6. Expect days or weeks. The website, ads and other sources carry the leads meanwhile.

Keep an evidence folder ready from the day the profile is verified.
