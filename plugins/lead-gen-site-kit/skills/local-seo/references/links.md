# Links: earning authority honestly

A new local site does not need thousands of links. On a previous build, the page one result for the main city's head term held its spot with about 60 referring domains and very low domain authority. That is the realistic target: dozens of real, relevant links over a year.

## First, give people something to link to

Nobody chooses to link to a pricing page or a location page in an article. Publish two or three genuinely useful pages first (see `build-site` content reference): a printable checklist, a cost guide with a real price table, a rules or rights explainer per region. Each with its own URL. These make outreach possible.

## Where real local links come from

In rough order of value for effort:

1. **Business relationships that also produce bookings.** On a previous build the best target was the businesses that refer this work every week: they want one reliable number to call, and a "customer resources" link follows the relationship. Find the equivalent in the niche (real estate agents, builders, strata managers, removalists). Pitch the service, not the link.
2. **Chambers of commerce and business associations.** Member directories usually carry a followed link. Pick ones genuinely near the business.
3. **Local sponsorship.** Sponsoring a junior or amateur club is a donation with a link attached, not a link scheme. Check first that existing sponsor links are not `nofollow` or `sponsored` if the link is the main reason.
4. **Data and helpful content for publishers.** Consumer and housing sites will not link to a tradie, but they will cite a useful dataset or checklist. Offer data and a named spokesperson quote, not a guest post, and do not ask for a link in the first email.
5. **Journalist request services** (in Australia, SourceBottle). Answer relevant call-outs with a named spokesperson. The journalist does not filter by domain authority.
6. **Student and community offers.** A standing discount listed on a university or community discounts page, where the audience genuinely uses the service.
7. **Guest posts on quality sites.** A useful article on a real site with real traffic (1,000 or more visitors a month is a fair bar) and readers who could use the service, linking to the most relevant service page. Paying for the placement is fine; check the site's own traffic and that it is not a link farm first.
8. **A quality press release.** A real piece of news (a launch, a new area, a community project) on a reputable local or industry release service, carrying the brand name, details and web address. It earns brand mentions and news links more than ranking power on its own.
9. **Brand authority and news links.** Links that point at the brand's own listings and social profiles, and news site mentions of the brand, help Google tie the details together.

## Finding prospects, step by step

When the user asks for link prospects, produce a short list they can act on this week:

1. Ask who the contractor buys from and which associations or networks they belong to. Those relationships come first.
2. Search for ten prospects local to the business: council and community business directories, chambers of commerce, trade associations, suppliers of the materials used, local clubs that list sponsors, businesses in neighbouring trades.
3. Open each one and confirm it is live, local and lists businesses. Drop link farms and sites that exist only to sell links.
4. For each, give the page where businesses are listed, how to get listed, and a short, plain email the user can send in their own name.
5. Ask for the brand name or the plain web address as the link text. Never keyword-stuffed text. Where a listing lets the user choose the address (a directory, an association or supplier listing), point some at the matching location or service page, not all at the home page.
6. Add each to the tracker with a follow-up date. Two or three new prospects a month is enough.

Expect a normal hit rate: one to three links from 20 to 30 pitches. Track replies weekly; links lag.

Do not pitch government regulators for links. They will not link to a commercial business.

## Never do this

- **Link networks and bulk packages:** private blog networks, tier 2 link packages, Google stacks, and gigs that promise hundreds of links. At a small link count, a burst of network links is a large and obvious share of the profile. A few links from real sites beat many from a network.
- **Reciprocal link rings** with other businesses in the same niche. Trivial to detect.
- **Directory blasts.**
- **A pre-emptive disavow file** on a new site. With no bad links there is nothing to disavow, and it can suppress good ones.
- **Point anyone who built spammy links for a related brand at this domain.**

## Measure

Track referring domains monthly (Bing Webmaster Tools inbound links is free; Ahrefs or similar if available). Add a line to the project changelog for every link earned.
