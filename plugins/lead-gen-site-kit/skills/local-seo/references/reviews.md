# Reviews: the lever that works on a brand new domain

Map pack prominence for a service-area business is driven heavily by review count and recency, not by domain authority. That makes reviews the one channel where a site with zero backlinks can compete within a few months. On a previous build, the map pack for the head term in its main city was held by businesses with 150 to 900 reviews; that is the real target, not a backlink number.

## The rules (consumer law and Google policy)

These apply in Australia (ACCC guidance and the Australian Consumer Law, plus the Spam Act) and have close equivalents in most countries. Check the local rules for other markets.

- **Ask every completed customer.** No gating: do not ask only the happy ones, and do not route unhappy customers to a private form first.
- **No incentives** for reviews, and no buying reviews.
- **No fake, staff-written or imported reviews on the profile.** Reviews on a profile come only from that profile's own customers.
- **No reviews from friends, family or other course members.** They breach the platform's rules and consumer law, and they are easy to detect.
- **No batch loading.** A sudden burst on a dormant profile is a suspension flag. Aim for a steady few a week.
- **Every SMS and email ask is a commercial electronic message**: it needs consent (a completed, paid job generally supports inferred consent), clear sender identity, and a working unsubscribe in every message, honoured promptly.
- **Never publish a rating on the website that your real reviews do not support.**

## The ask flow

1. Get the short review link from the Google profile.
2. In the CRM (GoHighLevel on a previous build), trigger an SMS and an email to every completed job, sent when the customer has the result (if the result is confirmed later, for example by an inspection, send it after that, not straight after the job).
3. One follow-up a few days later, then stop.

For a lead network that only knows about the enquiry, not the finished job, time the ask from the enquiry: one message about 30 days later, asking how it went with the contractor they were connected to. Tell the contractor the message goes out. One message, no follow-up.
4. If the Google profile question is not settled yet, point the ask at review platforms that survive either outcome (for Australia, ProductReview.com.au or Word of Mouth) and switch the Google link on once the profile is confirmed.

## Responding

Respond to every review, good and bad, within a day or two. Mention the service and place naturally.

- Positive: "Thanks {name}. So glad the {service} in {suburb} went smoothly. Enjoy the result."
- Negative: "Thanks for the honest feedback, {name}, and sorry this fell short. That is not our standard. Please contact us on {phone} so we can make it right."

## Using reviews on the site

A lead network with no reviews of its own yet may show the real reviews of the contractor who does the work, on the website only, when three things are true: the contractor has agreed in writing, each review is copied word for word, and the section is clearly labelled as reviews of the local contractor who does the work. Never present them as reviews of the brand, and never add rating schema for them.

- Show real reviews with name, location and their real date.
- Mark up `aggregateRating` and `Review` schema only from these real reviews, with real dates.
- Update the site's displayed rating when the real numbers change. Never compute review dates at build time.

## Targets

First milestone: 10 recent reviews with the newest inside the last week. Then hold three to five a week indefinitely. Track count and date of newest review weekly.
