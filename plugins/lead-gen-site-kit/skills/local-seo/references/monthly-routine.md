# The monthly routine

What to do after launch, month by month. Use this when the user asks "what do I do now?" or "what should I do for SEO this month?". Work out which month they are in, give them that month's list, and do as much of it with them as you can in the session.

Set the expectation first: on a new domain, expect little movement for two to three months. That is normal, and it is why paid ads cover the gap. Steady, small and real beats a burst.

No rank tracker is needed. Search Console's average position for a handful of main searches is enough.

## Before month 1

Three things block most of the list below. If any is missing, say so first, help the user get it, and give them only the tasks that can go ahead without it (Search Console, Bing, the review of pages, link prospect research).

- A real phone number.
- The business number, published on the site.
- The master details block, final.

If you cannot open websites in this session, do not name a directory, a prospect or a competitor's category as if you had checked it. Give the user the exact search to run and what to look for, and work from what they report back.

## Month 1: foundations (4 to 6 hours)

- [ ] The master details block is final (see the `local-seo` skill).
- [ ] The sitemap is submitted in Google Search Console and in Bing Webmaster Tools. Bing matters because several AI assistants read from its index.
- [ ] Bing Places and Apple Business Connect are set up.
- [ ] The Google profile is complete (see `google-business-profile.md`). For a lead network this is the owner's decision: read them the "Lead networks and the profile" section of the skill first, and skip this line if they decide against it.
- [ ] The first ten core listings are live (see `citations.md`). Before recommending any directory, open it and confirm it is live and still accepts listings. Stop at about twenty in total.
- [ ] Social profiles are live with the same details, including a company page on LinkedIn.
- [ ] The review request is running (see `reviews.md`).
- [ ] A tracker exists: every listing, the address submitted, status, and where the login is stored.

## Month 2 (2 to 3 hours)

- [ ] Ten local link prospects found and the first three contacted (see `links.md`).
- [ ] The Search Console review below, with one or two pages improved.
- [ ] New real photos added to the profile.
- [ ] Every review replied to.

## Month 3 (2 to 3 hours)

- [ ] Link prospects followed up, two or three new ones added.
- [ ] One helpful page added if the data supports it (see "Adding a page" below).
- [ ] The AI answers check below.
- [ ] The first quarterly audit with the `audit-site` skill, saved with its date.

## Every month after (about 2 hours)

- [ ] The five-minute numbers check: clicks, impressions and average position for the main searches in Search Console, and leads for the month. Note what moved.
- [ ] Reply to reviews. Add photos to the profile.
- [ ] One improvement from the Search Console review.
- [ ] Two or three new link prospects.

## Every quarter (1 extra hour)

- [ ] Run `audit-site`, compare with the last dated report, do the next five fixes.
- [ ] Recheck the contractor's details: licence, areas, hours, phone. Sites go stale when the business changes.
- [ ] Decide the bigger moves from the numbers: more suburbs, a second profile, a second city.

## The Search Console review: improve what is nearly ranking

Ask the user to export the last three months of performance data (queries and pages).

1. Find queries where a page sits between position 8 and 20 with a meaningful number of impressions. Those are the targets.
2. For the best three, name the page and what is holding it back. The usual causes: the phrase is missing from the title or a heading, the page does not answer the question directly, or no other page links to it.
3. Propose the exact change for each. Never invent facts about the business to fill a gap; ask.
4. Make one or two changes, log them with the date, and check the same queries in four weeks. Changing everything at once shows nothing.
5. With low traffic the numbers are noisy. Do not react to one week.

## Adding a page

Add a page only when Search Console shows people searching for something the business serves and no page answers it. Three kinds are worth adding: an area served, a service offered, or a helpful page answering a common question (cost, council rules, how long it takes).

Before writing an area page, state what is specific to that place that is not on the other pages, and ask for local facts if there are none. If swapping the place name would change nothing else, tell the user not to build it. Add areas in small batches and watch the result before adding more. Never add a page for a place or service the contractor does not cover.

## The AI answers check

1. Confirm AI crawlers are allowed to read the site.
2. List the five questions customers most often ask about the service in that place.
3. For each, find where the site answers it in two or three plain, visible sentences with specifics. Answers hidden in click-to-open boxes are often missed.
4. Draft any missing answer using only facts already on the site or confirmed by the user.
5. Have the user ask two or three AI assistants the main customer question and note who is mentioned. Repeat each quarter.

Be straight with the user: this area is still changing, the steps above are sound practice, and nobody can guarantee a place in AI answers.
